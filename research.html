Research

Briefly stated, my research examines shifts in democratic policy regarding noncitizen voting rights across Western democracies. Some nations have expanded voting access to noncitizen migrants, while others have tightened participation criteria. My papers investigate whether and when citizens believe noncitizens should be allowed to vote in elections.

Published Research

Integration as a Precondition for Enfranchisement? Swiss Public Opinion on Noncitizen Voting Rights

Swiss Political Science Review 0(0): 1–31. · doi.org/10.1111/spsr.70005

This study identifies three types of criteria for inclusion: civic-cultural knowledge, economic contribution, and good character. Findings reveal partisanship as a primary factor shaping citizens' views on integration prerequisites for voting rights.

(Un)Conditional? The Role of Integration and Psychological Predispositions in Citizens' Attitudes towards Immigrant Enfranchisement

Conditional Accept, Political Studies

A multi-country survey (2021) from Denmark, Germany, Sweden, Switzerland, the United Kingdom, and the United States examines how authoritarianism, social dominance orientation, and ethnocentrism shape views on immigrant political inclusion.

Works in Progress

Conditional inclusion into the polity: public opinion of regional immigrant enfranchisement policies

A conjoint survey experiment conducted in Switzerland, Sweden, and the US (late 2024), evaluating policy packages varying residency requirements, permit types, language proficiency, civic exams, and economic contributions.
